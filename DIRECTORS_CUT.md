# DIRECTORS_CUT.md

## The Experience, Reimagined

---

## One Principle

Every frame must earn its existence.

If a moment doesn't create tension, release tension, or hold tension — it doesn't exist.

If an image doesn't reveal something the previous image couldn't — it doesn't exist.

If a word doesn't say something the user couldn't have said themselves — it doesn't exist.

---

## The Journey

### ACT I: THE AWAKENING

**Why this act exists:** To make the user feel something before they understand anything. To create a question in their mind: "What is this?" That question is the only thing that matters in the first act. If the user doesn't ask it, the experience has already failed.

---

**BEAT 1 — The First Breath**
Progress: 0.00–0.04

Darkness. Pure, complete, total darkness.

No light point. No text. No particles. Nothing.

The user scrolls and sees nothing. For four percent of the scroll. This is not a mistake. This is the first test. The user who keeps scrolling is the user who is curious. The user who leaves was never the audience.

The darkness says: something is coming. You cannot see it yet. Trust me.

**Why darkness:** Because every meaningful experience begins with not knowing. A kitchen begins with an empty room. A meal begins with an empty plate. A story begins with silence. The darkness is not empty — it is full of potential. But the user cannot see that yet. They can only feel it.

**Why this length:** Four percent is approximately 320vh — about 35 wheel notches, or 6–8 seconds. This is the minimum duration required for the user to feel the absence of something. Shorter than this and it's a loading screen. Longer and it's a broken page.

---

**BEAT 2 — The Light**
Progress: 0.04–0.10

A single point of warm light appears at the exact center of the viewport.

It does not fade in. It ignites. One frame: darkness. Next frame: a small, warm, pulsing ember of light. The transition is instant — like a match being struck.

The light is small. Perhaps 20px. It pulses once — grows to 40px, shrinks to 25px, settles at 30px. The pulse takes two seconds. Then it holds.

Nothing else changes. The background is still darkness. There is no text. There are no particles. Just the light, alive, breathing, waiting.

**Why instant appearance:** A fade-in would be gentle. This is not a gentle moment. This is the first spark. It should feel like ignition — sudden, alive, warm. The user should feel that something just began.

**Why a pulse:** A static light is a dot. A pulsing light is a heartbeat. The pulse says: this is alive. This is not a UI element. This is something breathing in the dark.

**Why nothing else:** Because the light is the only thing that matters right now. If we add text, particles, or any other element, we dilute the light. The user's entire attention should be on this single point. This is where curiosity is born.

---

**BEAT 3 — The Wordmark**
Progress: 0.10–0.18

The light holds.

Then, letter by letter, from left to right:

K . . . I . . . T . . . S . . . E . . . R

Each letter takes 0.01 of progress to appear. Between letters, there is a gap of 0.005 — a breath. The letters do not fade in. They resolve. They go from blurred (10px) to sharp (0px) in an instant, like a camera finding focus.

When the full word "KITSER" is visible, it holds for 0.04 of progress. Nothing moves. Nothing fades. The word sits on darkness, lit by the warm light point behind it.

The light point does not grow during this beat. It stays at 30px. It is the light source for the word, not a competing element.

**Why letter by letter:** Because the brand name is the most important word in the experience. It deserves to be read, not glanced at. Letter-by-letter forces the user to read each character. It creates a micro-moment of anticipation between each letter. It makes the name feel assembled, not displayed.

**Why blur-to-sharp:** Because this is how focus works in the real world. Things don't fade into existence — they come into focus. The blur-to-sharp transition says: this was always here. You're just now seeing it clearly.

**Why a hold after assembly:** Because the user needs to see the complete word. If we immediately transition to the tagline, the wordmark never lands. The hold is the moment where the brand name becomes real.

---

**BEAT 4 — The Meaning**
Progress: 0.18–0.24

Beneath "KITSER," a single line of text resolves:

ALL ABOUT KITCHENS

Weight 300. Small. Tracking wide. It appears the same way — blur-to-sharp — but as a single unit, not letter by letter.

The tagline does not drift, scale, or animate. It resolves and holds.

The light point behind the wordmark grows slightly during this beat — from 30px to 50px. This is the first change in the light since it appeared. It says: now that you know what this is, let me show you more.

**Why weight 300:** Because the tagline should not compete with the wordmark. Weight 100 for the brand name, weight 300 for the tagline. The visual hierarchy is clear: the name is the destination, the tagline is the path.

**Why no animation on the tagline:** Because the wordmark had its moment of assembly. The tagline doesn't need to repeat that gesture. It should be quieter. It should feel like the natural completion of a thought: "KITSER... all about kitchens."

**Why the light grows:** Because the light is the experience's emotional barometer. When something good happens, the light grows. When something is about to happen, the light dims. The light growing here says: we've established what this is. Now we can begin.

### ACT II: THE MATERIALS

**Why this act exists:** To make the user feel the weight of what a kitchen is made of. Not to list materials. Not to label textures. To make the user understand — in their body, not their mind — that a kitchen is made of stone that took millions of years to form, wood that took decades to grow, steel that took precision to fold. The materials are not ingredients. They are arguments.

---

**BEAT 5 — Stone**
Progress: 0.24–0.34

The wordmark and tagline fade out. Not gently — they dissolve. The blur increases from 0 to 6px as the opacity drops to 0. The transition takes 0.03 of progress. This is faster than the wordmark's assembly. The wordmark was slow because it was arriving. The dissolve is fast because it is leaving.

Darkness returns. But not the opening darkness. This darkness has warmth — the background has shifted from #050505 to #0f0a08. The light point is now 80px. The user can feel the warmth.

Then: stone.

A texture of stone appears. Not labeled. Not named. The stone is positioned left of center — occupying 60% of the viewport width. It has an elliptical mask with soft edges. It is slightly blurred when it appears, and de-blurs over 0.04 of progress.

The stone does not zoom. The stone does not pan. The stone is still. The camera is still. The only movement is the de-blur — the stone coming into focus.

There is no text. There is no label. The user sees stone. That is all.

The stone holds in perfect clarity for 0.04 of progress. Then it begins to dissolve — not fade, dissolve. The edges break apart first, like erosion. The center holds longest. The dissolution takes 0.03 of progress.

**Why no zoom or pan:** Because stone does not move. Stone is the most still material on earth. If we add Ken Burns motion to stone, we betray its nature. Stone should be still. The only motion should be the camera finding it — the de-blur.

**Why left of center:** Because center placement says "look at this." Left-of-center placement says "this is part of something." The user's eye naturally drifts right, so the stone occupies the left and the user's attention fills the remaining space with anticipation.

**Why no label:** Because the texture speaks. If we write "STONE" on stone, we reduce the stone to an illustration of a word. If we show stone without a word, we force the user to feel it. The user who recognizes stone doesn't need the label. The user who doesn't recognize stone is better off not knowing — they'll feel the texture without the cognitive filter of a word.

**Why dissolution, not fade:** Because stone doesn't fade. Stone erodes. The edge-first dissolution mimics how stone actually ages. It also creates visual variety — not every exit should be a uniform opacity fade.

---

**BEAT 6 — Copper**
Progress: 0.34–0.44

Darkness again. But the background has warmed further — #0f0a08 to #141010. The light point is now 120px. The warmth is growing.

Then: copper.

A warm, metallic surface appears. Positioned right of center — the mirror of the stone's position. Different mask shape — tighter, more circular. The copper de-blurs as it appears.

But this time, there is movement. A slow, horizontal pan — the copper surface slides from right to left at 0.5% per frame. The movement is almost imperceptible. The user doesn't consciously see it moving. But if they look away and look back, the copper is in a slightly different position. This creates a feeling of life — the copper is not a photograph, it is a surface catching light.

The copper also has a color shift. As it de-blurs, a warm overlay fades in — not the generic warm tint used on every image in the previous version. This is a specific copper-toned overlay that makes the surface feel like it's reflecting warm light from an unseen source.

There is no text. There is no label. The copper holds for 0.03 of progress, then dissolves from edges inward.

**Why different position:** Because the stone was left, the copper is right. This creates spatial rhythm — the user's eye learns that materials appear in different locations, which creates anticipation: "where will the next one appear?"

**Why movement:** Because copper is alive. Copper catches light differently from every angle. The slow pan makes the copper feel like it's reflecting different light as the "camera" moves. This is the first movement in the materials sequence. It should feel like the experience is waking up.

**Why color shift:** Because copper has a specific warmth that stone doesn't. The overlay is not decorative — it communicates material properties. Stone is cool, copper is warm. The user should feel this difference without being told.

---

**BEAT 7 — Steel**
Progress: 0.44–0.54

Darkness. Background: #141010. Light: 160px. The warmth is building.

Then: steel.

A cool, metallic surface. Centered — the first centered material. Tight elliptical mask. The steel de-blurs.

This time, the movement is vertical — a slow upward drift, like the steel is being lifted. The motion is even slower than the copper: 0.3% per frame. Barely there. But the vertical direction contrasts with the copper's horizontal, creating a sense of three-dimensional space.

The color overlay is cool — a slight blue-grey tint. The contrast with the warm copper is deliberate. The user's eye has been adapting to warmth. The steel should feel like a surprise — a cool note in a warm composition.

The steel holds for 0.03 of progress. Then, as it dissolves, a single number appears at the center of the viewport:

**0.1**

Weight 100. Massive — clamp(6rem, 18vw, 14rem). The number sits on pure darkness. No image. No texture. Just the number and the darkness.

The number pulses once — scale from 1.0 to 1.02 to 1.0. The pulse takes 0.02 of progress. Then it holds for 0.02. Then it dissolves.

**Why 0.1:** Because 0.1mm is the edge of Kitser's steel knives. This is not a specification — it's a statement. 0.1mm is thinner than a human hair. The number alone, on darkness, with no context, creates a question: "0.1 what?" The user has to lean in. They have to care enough to wonder. This is how you make someone interested in precision — not by telling them about precision, but by making them feel the weight of a single number.

**Why pulse:** Because the number should feel alive. A static number is data. A pulsing number is a heartbeat. The pulse says: this number matters. It's not decoration. It's the soul of what we do.

**Why no label:** Because "0.1mm edge" would reduce the number to a spec. "0.1" alone is poetry. The user who knows what it means will feel the precision. The user who doesn't will feel the mystery. Both reactions are correct.

### ACT III: THE CRAFT

**Why this act exists:** To bridge the gap between material and meaning. To show that a kitchen is not just ingredients — it is intention. Materials become a kitchen through craft. This act must make the user feel the patience, the precision, the human hand that transforms raw material into something sacred.

---

**BEAT 8 — Hands**
Progress: 0.54–0.64

The "0.1" dissolves.

Darkness. The background has shifted — #141010 to #181412. Still dark, but the warmth is more present. The light point has grown to 200px.

Then: hands.

Not a full image. Not a face. Just hands. Working with material. The image is cropped tight — we see texture, movement, intention. The hands are positioned in the lower third of the viewport. The upper two-thirds are darkness.

The image de-blurs as it appears. There is no zoom. No pan. The hands move because they are in the photograph — they are doing something. We don't see what. We don't need to. The act of making is the message.

Beneath the hands, in the lower third:

Crafted.

Weight 100. Small. Tracking wide. The word appears after the hands are in focus — a beat delay. The word doesn't announce the image. The image announces itself. The word confirms what the user already felt.

**Why hands:** Because hands are the human element. A kitchen is not a product — it is a relationship between material and maker. Hands make that relationship visible. Hands say: someone made this. Someone cared.

**Why cropped:** Because a full image of a person working would be a stock photo. Cropped hands are intimate. The user is close enough to see the work but not the worker. This creates mystery and respect — we don't know who these hands belong to, but we know they matter.

**Why "Crafted" not "CRAFT":** Because "CRAFT" is a noun — a category. "Crafted" is a verb — an action. It says this was done. By someone. With intention. The past tense is important — it says the work is already complete. The kitchen already exists. We're just showing you how it got there.

---

**BEAT 9 — The Assembly**
Progress: 0.64–0.74

"Crafted" dissolves. The hands image dissolves.

Darkness again. Light: 250px. Background: #1a1410.

Then: a word appears. Center. Large. Weight 100.

JOINERY

The word holds for 0.02 of progress. Then, beneath it:

The joints. The angles. The precision.

These three lines appear one at a time, each with a blur-to-sharp transition. Weight 300. Small. Each holds for 0.01 of progress after appearing.

Then all text dissolves.

A new image appears — a close-up of cabinet hardware. Tight. Intimate. The image is positioned center, occupying 50% of the viewport. De-blurs as it appears. Holds for 0.03 of progress. Dissolves.

**Why this sequence:** Because joinery is the hidden craft. Nobody sees the joints inside a cabinet. Nobody admires the angles. But the kitchen's integrity depends on them. This beat says: we care about what you can't see. The text introduces the concept. The image proves it. The dissolution says: we showed you. Now move on. We don't need to dwell.

**Why three lines of text:** Because three is the minimum for a rhythm. One line is a statement. Two lines is a contrast. Three lines is a pattern. "The joints. The angles. The precision." Each line is shorter than the last. This creates a visual deceleration — the rhythm slows down as the words become more precise.

---

**BEAT 10 — The Kitchen**
Progress: 0.74–0.88

The hardware image dissolves.

Darkness.

The longest darkness since the opening. From 0.74 to 0.76 — two percent of pure darkness. This is the held breath. The user has been seeing materials, hands, hardware. They've been reading words. Now: nothing. The darkness says: something is about to happen.

Then: the kitchen.

A full-bleed image. No mask. No ellipse. The entire viewport is the kitchen. The image de-blurs slowly — over 0.06 of progress, not 0.04 like the materials. The slower de-blur says: this is different. This is the destination.

The kitchen is lit warmly. The light point behind it has grown to 350px — the largest it has been. The warmth is at its peak.

The kitchen holds in perfect clarity for 0.06 of progress. This is the longest hold of any image in the experience. The user has time to see everything — the surfaces, the light, the space, the possibility. They have time to imagine themselves in this kitchen. They have time to want it.

No text appears over the kitchen. The image speaks.

After the hold, the kitchen begins to dissolve. But not to darkness — to a second kitchen. The dissolve is slow, 0.04 of progress. The two kitchens overlap briefly, creating a dreamlike transition. The user sees one kitchen fade into another, like memory into imagination.

The second kitchen holds for 0.04 of progress. Then it dissolves to a third — a kitchen with life in it. Not a staged photograph. A kitchen that looks like someone just left the room. A mug on the counter. Light through a window. The lived-in quality is the point.

The third kitchen holds for 0.04 of progress. Then it dissolves — not to another image, but to darkness.

**Why three kitchens:** Because one kitchen is a product. Three kitchens are a story. The first kitchen is the aspiration — perfect, pristine, aspirational. The second kitchen is the space — functional, warm, livable. The third kitchen is the life — imperfect, real, human. The progression from aspiration to life is the emotional arc of the entire brand.

**Why no text over the kitchens:** Because text over a kitchen turns the kitchen into an illustration. The kitchen should be the destination — the thing the user has been scrolling toward. If we add text, we dilute the arrival. The user should look at the kitchen and think "I want that." Not "I want that, and also that text is interesting."

**Why the held breath before the first kitchen:** Because anticipation is more powerful than revelation. If the kitchen appears immediately after the last material, it's just another image. If the user waits in darkness — even for two percent — the kitchen becomes an event. The darkness creates the tension. The kitchen releases it.

### ACT IV: THE INVITATION

**Why this act exists:** To give the user somewhere to go. Not a contact form. Not a phone number. A feeling. The feeling of: this is for me. This is where I belong. The invitation is not a call to action. It is a call to feeling.

---

**BEAT 11 — The Light Returns**
Progress: 0.88–0.94

The third kitchen dissolves to darkness.

The background transitions — from #1a1410 to #f5f0eb. The transition is slow, 0.04 of progress. The darkness becomes warmth becomes light. The light point, which has been growing throughout the experience, now fills the viewport — it has become the background. The light has won.

On this light background, a single word:

LIGHT

Weight 100. Massive. The word is not centered — it is positioned slightly above center, creating visual weight below it. The space below the word is intentional — it is where the next thought will appear.

The word resolves blur-to-sharp. Holds for 0.03 of progress. This is the first word on a light background in the entire experience. The contrast with the preceding darkness is the visual equivalent of stepping outside on a spring morning. The user should feel the temperature change.

**Why "LIGHT" on light:** Because light is both the subject and the environment. The word sits in the thing it describes. This is not a label — it is a self-referential statement. Light is here. You are in it.

---

**BEAT 12 — The Statement**
Progress: 0.94–1.00

"LIGHT" dissolves — slowly, over 0.02 of progress. The light background holds.

Then:

A KITCHEN
IS NOT FURNITURE.

Two lines. Weight 100 for the first, weight 900 for the second. The second line — "IS NOT FURNITURE." — is the heavier thought. It is not highlighted or colored differently. It is simply heavier. The weight does the work.

The two lines appear together — no stagger. They are one thought, not two. They resolve blur-to-sharp. Hold for 0.03 of progress.

Then they dissolve.

In their place:

THE KITCHEN
IS WHERE
LIFE HAPPENS.

Three lines. Weight 100, weight 100, weight 900. The progression of weight tells the story: the kitchen (light) is where (light) LIFE HAPPENS (heavy). The emphasis is on the last two words. They are the destination of the entire experience.

These three lines appear with stagger — "THE KITCHEN" first, then "IS WHERE" one beat later, then "LIFE HAPPENS" one beat after. Each line resolves blur-to-sharp. The stagger creates a sentence that unfolds in real time, like someone speaking.

"LIFE HAPPENS" holds for 0.04 of progress. This is the longest hold of any text element. The user should read it, feel it, and carry it with them.

Then the text dissolves.

**Why "IS NOT FURNITURE" in weight 900:** Because this is the brand's philosophical position. Kitser is not selling furniture. A kitchen is not a product you buy. It is a space you inhabit. The weight says: we believe this. We're not being decorative. This is our truth.

**Why "LIFE HAPPENS" is the climax:** Because everything before this — the darkness, the light, the materials, the craft, the kitchens — leads to this. The kitchen is not about stone or steel or joinery. It is about what happens inside it. Life. The word "LIFE" in weight 900 is the emotional peak. The user should feel it.

**Why stagger on the final statement:** Because the stagger forces the user to read at the experience's pace, not their own. "THE KITCHEN" — pause — "IS WHERE" — pause — "LIFE HAPPENS." The pauses create rhythm. The rhythm creates emphasis. The emphasis creates meaning.

---

**BEAT 13 — The Close**
Progress: 1.00

"LIFE HAPPENS" dissolves.

The light background holds.

The light point — which has been growing for the entire experience — now contracts. It shrinks from filling the viewport to a 60px point at center. The contraction is slow, 0.02 of progress. The light is returning to its origin. The journey is complete.

On the light background, beneath the contracting light:

Kitser

Weight 100. The brand name, one final time. Not letter by letter. Not with blur. Just present. It resolves and holds.

Beneath it:

No. 1, Nava India Road, Coimbatore — 641028

Weight 300. Small. The address is the only practical information in the entire experience. It is placed at the very end because it is the least important thing — until the user wants it. Then it is the most important thing.

Both lines hold for 0.02 of progress. Then the experience ends. The user is on a light background with the brand name and address. They can scroll no further. The journey is complete.

**Why the light contracts:** Because the journey began with a single point of light. It should end with one. The contraction says: the experience is over, but the light remains. The user carries it with them.

**Why no final animation:** Because the experience has earned its stillness. The last thing the user sees should be calm. Not a fade, not a dissolve, not a zoom. Just presence. The brand name, the address, the light. That is enough.

## What Was Removed and Why

| Removed | Why |
|---------|-----|
| Particles | Decorative. They don't create emotion. They create noise. |
| Film grain | Decorative. It's a filter, not a feeling. |
| Progress bar | Functional but distracting. The experience should be immersive enough that the user forgets they're scrolling a website. |
| Nav | The nav has nowhere to go. The experience is the destination. |
| Top-to-bottom scroll to begin prompt | The darkness IS the prompt. If the user doesn't know to scroll, they're not the audience. |
| Multiple warm tint overlays | Each material should have its own color language. A universal warm tint erases material specificity. |
| Uniform blur-in/scale/fade envelope | Every element should have its own animation vocabulary based on its material properties. |
| "ALL ABOUT KITCHENS" tagline | Replaced by the materials themselves. The user doesn't need to be told Kitser is about kitchens. They should feel it. |
| Category labels (JOINERY, CABINET SYSTEMS, STORAGE) | These are navigation labels, not emotional content. The images communicate these concepts without words. |
| Spec cascade (72 layers, of folded steel, 0.1mm edge) | Replaced by the "0.1" moment — a single number that creates curiosity instead of delivering information. |

## The Emotional Map

Progress:  0.00 --- 0.04 --- 0.10 --- 0.18 --- 0.24 --- 0.34 --- 0.44 --- 0.54 --- 0.64 --- 0.74 --- 0.88 --- 0.94 --- 1.00
Emotion:   |← DARK →|← SPARK →|← NAME →|← MEANING →|← STONE →|← COPPER →|← STEEL →|← HANDS →|← CRAFT →|← KITCHEN →|← LIGHT →|← WORDS →|← CLOSE →|
Intensity: ░░░░░░░░░ ▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ ▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ ▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ ██████████ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ ██████████ ▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒

The experience is not a smooth gradient. It is a series of peaks and valleys. The darkness creates valleys. The reveals create peaks. The holds create plateaus. The dissolves create descents. The rhythm is: valley, peak, plateau, descent, valley, peak, plateau, descent — with the kitchen as the highest peak and the climax as the emotional summit.

## The Test

After watching the complete experience, if there is any point where the user could say "okay I understand" before the end — the experience has failed.

The user should never understand. They should only feel.

Understanding comes later — when they visit the showroom, when they talk to someone, when they choose their kitchen. The website's job is not to inform. It is to make someone care enough to find out more.

If the user finishes scrolling and thinks "that website has nice animations" — the experience has failed.

If the user finishes scrolling and thinks "I've never seen a kitchen website like that" — the experience has succeeded.

If the user finishes scrolling and feels something they can't quite name — the experience has exceeded.
